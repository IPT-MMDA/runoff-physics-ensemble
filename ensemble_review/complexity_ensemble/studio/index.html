RESEARCH / LOW-DATA TRANSFER

Discover a shared relationship.

Distil a source-trained neural runoff process into a symbolic approximation, then calibrate three local parameters in data-scarce catchments.

Snow and water storage evolve explicitly. KAN approximates the teacher’s daily runoff fraction; its equation structure is frozen before target adaptation. Source, source-validation and target catchments must be disjoint. This tests symbolic transfer, without claiming discovery of a universal physical law.

This study uses CAMELS-CH. Complete daily weather, including spin-up, is required. Missing discharge labels are supported. Low-data regimes use the first N available training-period discharge labels; all other target discharge labels are hidden during calibration. Weather drives continuous simulation without discharge assimilation. Results are hindcasts with observed weather.

Simulated forecast-related costs

        

          
How do we calculate the savings?

          
    
            
  1. Compare the same days. We pair A and B with the same observed river flow, catchment and forecast issue/lead. Both models are scored on exactly the same usable data.
    2. 
            
  2. Turn flow into daily energy. Using the head, efficiency and flow limits entered above, we calculate energy from each prediction and from the observed flow. The latter is our estimate of available energy.
    3. 
            
  3. Price the energy error. Predicting more energy than was available incurs the shortfall penalty; predicting less incurs the surplus penalty. We multiply each energy difference in MWh by its selected €/MWh penalty and add the costs across the paired period.
    4. 
            
  4. Compare spending with B. Savings = cost B − cost A. Savings (%) = 100 × (cost B − cost A) ÷ cost B. A positive result means A costs less under this scenario; a negative result means A costs more.
    5. 
          

          
Example: if B's simulated cost is €100 and A's is €80, A saves €20, or 20%. Its cost index is 80 when B = 100. This percentage concerns forecast-related penalties, not the plant's total operating budget. When B costs zero, the percentage and index are undefined.

          

            Assumptions behind the estimate
            
    
              
  • A simplified run-of-river plant. We use basin gauge flow as a proxy for turbine inflow, with the same chosen head, efficiency, environmental flow and turbine-flow limit for A and B. These settings describe an abstract plant, not measured properties of each mapped plant.
    • 
              
  • Constant flow within each day. Each daily mean stands for 96 identical quarter-hours. With constant head and efficiency, E = 0.23544 × efficiency × head (m) × turbine flow (m³/s), in MWh/day. Turbine flow is reduced by environmental flow and bounded by zero and the optional maximum. Daily averaging makes these flow limits approximate.
    • 
              
  • Assumed prices. The editable penalties are illustrative, not historical market prices or industry-average fees. We do not simulate intraday variability, reservoir scheduling, outages or actual market settlement.
    • 
              
  • One abstract plant per matched basin. Several map markers can share one basin's results; we count that basin once. Catchments can overlap, so totals describe the abstract comparison cohort, not an actual fleet's electricity production or spending.
    • 
              
  • The selected period only. Costs cover all paired days in the selected range, not just the map date, and are not annualized. Available energy is calculated from observed flow, not measured generation. MAE and NSE describe accuracy separately; we do not convert an NSE increase directly into euros.
